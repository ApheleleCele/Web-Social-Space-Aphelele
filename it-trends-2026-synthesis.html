A critical synthesis for 2025–2026, with reflection on the South African context

Top 5 Hot Trends in IT

Prepared for ISTN731 — Special Topics in Information Systems, Honours Programme (IS&T), University of KwaZulu-Natal. This page is my own synthesis of where Gartner, Deloitte and Forrester's 2026 forecasts genuinely agree, where their analysts openly disagree with each other, and what each trend actually means once it lands in South Africa — under POPIA, our infrastructure realities, and a cybercrime landscape that Interpol now ranks us top of in Africa.

Aphelele Cele · August 2026 · Honours, Information Systems & Technology

Method

Why these five, and why cross-check three analyst houses at once

Technology trend forecasting is now central to organisational strategy in an AI-driven, more connected digital world. Rather than leaning on a single source, this synthesis triangulates three major 2026 industry reports — Gartner, Deloitte and Forrester — on the basis that convergence across independent analysts is a stronger signal than any one report alone. Where useful, it also draws on ideas raised by classmates on the ISTN731 module forum, several of whom independently flagged the same patterns: Physical AI, Agentic AI and Confidential Computing.

Each of the five trends below appears in at least two of the three 2026 reports and is corroborated by further sources — news reporting, expert interviews, or vendor data. For each one I set out (1) what it is and why it qualifies, (2) at least two independent sources of evidence, (3) a look at where experts genuinely disagree — on maturity, risk, or business value — rather than a flattened consensus, and (4) how it is actually landing in South Africa, a country that ITWeb (2026)16 ranks as the continent's leading adopter of generative AI, and that operates under its own regulatory layer — POPIA and the draft National AI Policy.

01 / 05

Physical AI (AI-Powered Robotics)

Definition & why it qualifies

Physical AI moves artificial intelligence off the screen and into machines that see, reason and act in the real world in real time — robots, drones, self-driving vehicles and smart industrial tools. It is the trend that appears most consistently across all three 2026 reports reviewed here. Gartner [9] places it under its "Synthesist" theme: intelligence that gives robots, drones and smart machines a direct, operational impact. Deloitte [4] devotes a full chapter to it, arguing intelligence is "going physical" through fleets of robots, autonomous vehicles and smart devices already handling real operational load — and that leaders now need to manage these systems as a fleet, not a set of isolated pilots. Forrester [6] agrees that AI has moved out of pure software and into physical space.

Where the experts differ
Governance-first vs. value-first. Gartner reads Physical AI mainly through infrastructure and governance — structure, safety, explainability — implying caution about deploying real robots without proper management in place. Deloitte takes a more practical, already-happening view: autonomous forklifts, robotic picking arms and inspection drones are in production now, not on a roadmap. Both views reconcile once you note that Physical AI's risk is context-dependent — a warehouse robot in a controlled setting carries far less governance burden than an autonomous vehicle navigating a public street.
Relevance to South Africa
Deloitte Africa's 2026 regional analysis [5] argues Physical AI suits African manufacturing, mining and logistics precisely because those environments are controlled and can show measurable value quickly — bounded deployments over open-ended ones. NTT DATA South Africa has already partnered with manufacturers to fold Physical AI into production lines as part of the country's Industry 4.0 push [20][21]. Mining, with its remote operations, hazardous conditions and high-value assets, is a particularly viable setting locally for autonomous inspection and equipment operation [5].
02 / 05

Agentic AI & Multiagent Systems

Definition & why it qualifies

IBM [10] defines Agentic AI as systems that can plan, execute and revise multi-step processes without human intervention, while multiagent systems coordinate specialised agents to complete complex tasks collaboratively. Gartner [9] names Multiagent Systems a leading 2026 strategic trend for scaling automation; Deloitte [4] frames the shift as organisations increasingly managing AI agents as "a silicon-based staff" rather than a replacement for people; Forrester [6] treats multi-agent systems as foundational "build-layer" infrastructure for 2026.

IBM Technology (2026). Multi AI agent systems: when one AI brain isn't enough. [11]

A genuine point of disagreement
Overhyped, or a normal shakeout? In June 2025, Gartner analyst Anushree Verma predicted that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, citing rising costs, unclear business value and weak management control — and coined "agent-washing" for vendors relabelling ordinary chatbots and RPA tools as "agentic" [8]. Gartner estimates only around 130 vendors offer genuinely agentic capability out of many that claim it. Forbes [24] echoes this, pointing to weak governance and a pilot-to-production gap rather than flawed underlying models.

Trullion [25], an AI-native accounting technology company, disagrees: it argues these projects are being written off against short-term ROI standards that historically also failed to capture the early value of electricity and cloud computing, and reads the 40% figure as a healthy shakeout of weak projects, not proof of overhype. Trullion has a commercial stake in that framing, which is worth weighing — but its historical comparison is a legitimate methodological point, not just marketing. The most defensible reading combines both: real long-term value is plausible, and Gartner's governance concerns are well-evidenced and shouldn't be dismissed because they're commercially inconvenient.
Relevance to South Africa
Lenovo Southern Africa's General Manager for Infrastructure Solutions expects South African companies to move from traditional automation to agentic AI by the end of 2026 — optimising blast patterns and ventilation in mining, or personalising retail experiences for shoppers using informal payment methods like lay-by [12]. At the same time, South Africa's own AI-powered cybercrime problem (see Trend 4) is exactly the governance failure mode Gartner warns about: attackers are already automating reconnaissance and large-scale social engineering [1]. The same autonomy that helps local businesses run more efficiently is being turned against them.
03 / 05

AI-Native Development Platforms

Definition & why it qualifies

These are software environments where generative and agentic AI is built into the whole development lifecycle — system design, coding, testing, deployment — rather than bolted on as a helper. Gartner [9] lists it as a top 2026 trend, noting platforms that let small, fast-moving teams ship with generative AI in ways that are becoming enterprise-ready. Forrester's "agentic software development" category [6] expects a sharp acceleration in delivery speed as agents generate and refine software components, while cautioning the technology still needs a few years before agent coordination and safety mature fully. This connects directly to how development teams are structured and how quickly they deliver — the core subject of my companion ISTN713 module.

Where the experts differ
Productivity boost, or an eroding training ground? Microsoft's Satya Nadella frames these tools as freeing developers for bigger, more creative work [15]. A more cautious view from Dariel's head of talent acquisition, Lisa Jasper, notes that the routine tasks AI now handles — boilerplate code, simple bug fixes, test creation — have long been how junior developers actually learn to spot and solve problems; automating them risks a generation of engineers who can prompt AI but can't independently verify its output [13]. A third, governance-focused view from AiBuzz [2] cites a 2026 study finding one in four AI-generated code samples carried a confirmed OWASP Top-10 security flaw — while only 24% of companies thoroughly review AI-generated code for IP, licensing, security and quality. The real risk may be inadequate review, not AI-assisted development itself.

South Africa's ICT sector contributes roughly 8% of national economic output and has been a real route into stable, well-paid work for graduates from less privileged backgrounds, against a youth unemployment rate above 45% [15]. If entry-level coding work keeps automating, Dariel and outlets including iAfrica and Intelligent CIO Africa warn the usual junior-to-senior developer pathway — a genuine route to the middle class for many South Africans — could weaken, unless training is deliberately redesigned around AI-based workflows rather than the old apprenticeship model [13][14].
04 / 05

AI Security & Trust Platforms

Definition & why it qualifies

These platforms unify security for everything an organisation's AI touches — models, prompts, agents, data movement, tool use — because that surface doesn't map onto conventional network or device security. Gartner [9] lists "AI Security Platforms" as a top 2026 trend, alongside "Preemptive Cybersecurity" — using AI to stop threats before they land rather than reacting afterwards. Fortinet [7] makes the defensive case: AI speeds up detection and response, adapts as new threats appear, and cuts analyst workload by automatically monitoring huge data volumes — a source also cited independently by a classmate on the module forum, which strengthens confidence it's genuinely load-bearing evidence rather than a single outlier claim.

Where the experts differ
Who does AI actually help more — defenders or attackers? Palo Alto Networks leadership says AI-driven security automation is tilting the balance to defenders: it reports that almost 70% of its customers have cut mean time to detect and respond to under 15 minutes because of AI [23]. Interpol's African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 tells a starker story: AI is now involved in 55% of reported African cybercrime cases, with losses more than doubling from $192 million (2024) to $484 million (2026), and AI used across the entire attack chain — reconnaissance, phishing, extortion, evasion [3][26]. Read together, AI is almost certainly accelerating both sides at once; whether an organisation ends up net more secure depends on how its investment in AI-driven defence compares to the AI-powered threat it actually faces — which is exactly why Gartner frames this as a platform-level need, not a single-tool purchase.

South Africa isn't a minor case study here — per Interpol, it's the primary target in Africa: 92% of the continent's reported ransomware cases and 70% of business email compromise incidents, plus 213,523 reported DDoS attacks (one peaking at 312 Gbps) in the 2026 African Cyberthreat Assessment Report [26]. Locally, SABRIC puts the annual cost of cybercrime at roughly R2.2 billion, with phishing responsible for 78% of digital banking fraud in 2025, and Accenture attributing 54% of local data breaches to identity theft [1]. Given that exposure — layered on South Africa being the continent's most digitally developed market — AI security and trust platforms are not theoretical here; Palo Alto Networks has cited this exact risk profile as a reason to expand its South African operations [23].
05 / 05

Confidential Computing

Definition & why it qualifies

Confidential computing protects sensitive data while it's actually being processed, not only at rest or in transit, using hardware-based trusted execution environments (TEEs) to create secure processing zones. It answers a growing problem as more organisations run AI workloads on shared or third-party cloud infrastructure. Gartner [9] places it in its "Architect" theme — technology that protects data in use and enables secure AI and analytics even on infrastructure you don't fully trust. Microsoft [18] has already operationalised it through Azure's confidential computing offering for digital sovereignty and regulated workloads — a sign this has moved from research concept to deployable product.

ServeTheHome (2025). Next-level security: confidential computing is going mainstream. [22]

Where the experts differ
How mature is it, really? This trend draws less open disagreement than the others, but real uncertainty remains about how settled it is. A classmate placed confidential computing on the "Slope of Enlightenment" in Gartner's Hype Cycle in a forum post — a reasonable but arguably conservative read. Since Azure, AWS Nitro Enclaves and Google Cloud Confidential VMs now all offer it as a standard, generally available service rather than a preview feature, it's arguably closer to the early "Plateau of Productivity" for its core use case — protecting data in use on major public clouds. Newer applications, like confidential multi-party AI training, are still genuinely early-stage. In practice: treat baseline confidential computing as well-understood and lower-risk, but stay cautious applying it to advanced AI training, which is still developing.
Relevance to South Africa
Confidential computing connects directly to South Africa's evolving data protection and sovereignty rules. POPIA requires that personal information transferred abroad be protected to a standard equivalent to local protection, and the National Policy on Data and Cloud (2024) requires data tied to national security or sovereignty to be hosted physically inside South Africa [17]. POPIA itself doesn't mandate local storage for all private-sector data, but the Prudential Authority — under the Banks Act — requires banks to run risk assessments and consult regulators before moving data offshore [19]. For regulated sectors — banking, healthcare, government — confidential computing offers a real way to meet these overlapping requirements while still using large-scale cloud infrastructure, because it protects data being processed, not just stored, at the hardware level, regardless of where the provider is physically located.
Closing synthesis

Five trends, one shared thread

Physical AI, Agentic AI & Multiagent Systems, AI-Native Development Platforms, AI Security & Trust Platforms, and Confidential Computing each earn their place on a 2026 Top 10 list through independent agreement across Gartner, Deloitte and Forrester, reinforced by outside reporting and, in places, by peer research from the ISTN731 forum. What separates a critical synthesis from simply repeating analyst copy is sitting with the places experts genuinely disagree: whether agentic AI's high failure rate signals overhype or a normal shakeout of badly managed projects; whether AI is helping cybersecurity defenders more than attackers; whether AI-native development threatens the junior developer pipeline or is just reshaping it. Nearly every trend here is AI-adjacent, which says a great deal about how much AI is currently shaping the technology horizon.

Across every trend, South Africa isn't passively absorbing global shifts — it's actively exposed, sometimes more than others. It leads Africa in generative AI adoption [16], and it's simultaneously the continent's leading cybercrime target. It also has its own regulatory layer — POPIA, the National Policy on Data and Cloud, and the draft National AI Policy — shaping exactly how each of these trends will actually play out here.
